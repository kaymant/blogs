TEAM 16RD1 · Empathize → Analyze
The problem we are investigating

Completion is visible. Understanding is not.

Students can use generative AI to get explanations, code, calculations and finished academic work — but successful completion does not prove independent understanding.
AI-supported performance≠independent mastery

Our design challenge is not to reduce AI use. It is to understand when AI supports learning, when it quietly replaces reasoning, and how students can recognise the difference.

~20 sec

Frame the core tension. The interviews consistently showed that students value AI. So the project is not an anti-AI intervention. The issue is visibility: a task can be complete while the learner still cannot reproduce the reasoning, transfer it, retain it, or verify it.

TEAM 16Empathize · cross-interview synthesis
Behaviour patterns emerging from the 12-interview Empathize phase

AI behaviour changes with the student’s goal and context

Learning-oriented use

  • Attempts first, then asks about the exact point of confusion.
  • Requests “why?”, derivations, shorter explanations and similar problems.
  • Checks steps against prior reasoning or course material.
  • Uses AI to create practice, quizzes or active speaking situations.

Completion-oriented use

  • Deadline pressure makes finishing the artifact the immediate goal.
  • Full code, reports or answers may be generated before understanding catches up.
  • The apparent ease of an explanation can be mistaken for mastery.
  • Verification and follow-up practice are often optional and delayed.

Five recurring learning risks

False mastery

2

Under pressure, learning goals can become completion goals.

Weak transfer

~20 sec

These conflicts are important because neither side is simply wrong. A student genuinely needs speed under deadlines. Full verification can be costly. Productive struggle can become unproductive frustration. Our solution must preserve the positive side of both values.

TEAM 16Analyze · Conflicts
End of Analyze phase

Our “How Might We” directions

✓

Mastery: How might we help students distinguish between following an AI explanation and applying the concept independently?

⏱

Timing: How might we provide AI assistance at the right moment and level without removing productive struggle?

↔

Transfer: How might we help students apply familiar concepts to unfamiliar or differently worded problems?

⌕

Trust: How might we let students check an AI explanation against their own course material quickly, in one place?

↻

Retention: How might we help students know, days later, whether they can still solve a similar problem on their own?

≋

Depth: How might we make AI explanations understandable at the learner’s current knowledge level without overwhelming them?

~45 sec

Present these as design directions, not features. Three came directly from my Canvas 2; the others are strengthened by Sushama’s analysis and Pawan’s three interviews with Sherya, Prashant and Abhay. The common thread is that the learner needs the right amount of support, evidence of independent ability, and lower-friction ways to verify and retain learning.

TEAM 16Solve phase plan
Possible prototype directions — hypotheses, not commitments

What might be worth exploring?

Guidance

Hint ladder / attempt-first flow — reveal only the next useful step before the full solution.

Mastery

Try-it-solo check — a similar problem after assistance, with AI temporarily withheld.

Transfer

Variation generator — change wording, context or concept combinations rather than repeat the same pattern.

Trust

Course-aware verification — link explanations back to notes, slides or trusted references.

Depth

Adaptive explanation depth — begin simple, then expand only when the learner needs more detail.

Retention

Delayed check-back — revisit concepts later with a short unassisted recall or transfer task.

TEAM 16Early solution hypotheses
What would success look like?

We should test learning behaviour, not just app satisfaction

Attempt

Does the student make a meaningful independent attempt before requesting a full solution?

Transfer

Can they solve a differently worded or mixed-concept problem after AI help?

Verify

Can they detect uncertainty or check an answer without leaving the learning flow?

Retain

Can they still explain or solve the concept after a delay?

~20 sec

This is our guardrail against designing another AI interface that merely feels helpful. The eventual prototype should produce observable evidence: more independent attempts, better transfer, easier verification and retained understanding.
